## Design choices
- `W()` recomputes `tt.block_to_dense(result.cores)` on each call (dense is fine per AGENTS.md);
  raises ValueError if the result is not a basis. `encode` uses `prior.apply_inv_sqrt`, `decode`
  and `V` use `prior.apply_sqrt` -- the square-L path is exactly the docstring formulas.
- `input_dis_tt` forces D = d: an explicit `D == d` is accepted, any other D raises ValueError;
  a prod(white_mode_sizes) / row mismatch raises. All other kwargs go to `dis_tt_dense`.

## OPEN ISSUE investigation: wide factor L  (RESULT: Q_TT is NOT a projector after truncation)
Setup (tests/test_input_side_extra.py, /tmp script reproduced in the test): toy problem,
L' = C^{1/2} Q with Q (64 x 128) having orthonormal rows, so C_X = L' L'^T unchanged,
white_dim 128 = 2^7, ker(L') has dimension 64. L^+ = L^T C^{-1}, P = L^+ L = projector onto
range(L^T). Snapshots: `input_dis_snapshots(eta_kind="basis", U_out=I)`.

Math: Q_TT = L W W^T L^+ gives Q_TT^2 = L W (W^T P W) W^T L^+, so (if L W has full column
rank) Q_TT is a projector iff W^T P W = I iff (I - P) W = 0, i.e. range(W) is orthogonal to ker(L).
This matches the docstring's condition. Likewise V^T C^{-1} V = W^T P W, so V is
E-orthonormal under the same condition only.

Findings:
| setting | r_X | ||(I-P)W||_2 | ||Q^2-Q||_2 | ||V^T C^-1 V - I||_2 | cert |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| full numerical rank | 8 | 1.4e-7 | 2.5e-14 | 2.0e-14 | 1.7e-16 |
| eps = 0.1 | 3 | 0.28 | 0.15 | 0.079 | 3.0e-3 |
| eps = 0.01 | 6 | 0.17 | 0.053 | 0.029 | 1.2e-5 |
| ranks (2,4,4,4,4,4,3) | 3 | 0.75 | 1.44 | 0.57 | 0.33 |
| ranks (2,4,8,8,8,8,3) | 3 | 0.63 | 0.88 | 0.39 | 3.6e-2 |
| ranks (..., 64, 70) | 70 | 1.00 | 1.25 | 1.00 | ~0 |

- Snapshots lie in range(L^T) to 6e-15, but the greedy TT frames do not: a final-bond column is
  w = W_d g with W_d = U_{<=d-1} (x) I, and Pi_{d-1} does not commute with P, so truncation at
  intermediate bonds pushes W partly into ker(L). Only the untruncated case is safe (its 1.4e-7
  is the ~sqrt(eps_mach)/gap accuracy of Gram eigenvectors).
- r_X > d_X necessarily intersects ker(L).
- Docstring remedy "project W onto range(L^T) first" (W' = orth(P W)), checked in the test only:
  restores Q^2 = Q to 1e-10 and never increases the training error (Z in range(P) gives
  ||W'^T Z|| >= ||W^T Z||). Measured relative training error: eps 0.1: 3.04e-3 -> 2.20e-3;
  eps 0.01: 1.23e-5 -> 8.15e-6; ranks (2,4,4,4,4,4,3): 0.328 -> 0.217. But W' = orth(P W)
  is dense in general (P has no TT structure here), so it gives up the TT form of W.
- Conclusion: wide L is a known limitation. The library does NOT support it: there is no
  detection or projection in `input_side.py`. A supported path needs a TT-structured P (or an
  ALS step constrained to range(L^T)); left for a follow-up/human decision.
  The extra test also covers a non-symmetric SQUARE factor, which works (all defects < 1e-10).
